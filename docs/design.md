# Boundary and state machine

CrossLedger is a bounded uniform-price call market over newly created abstract contract-native inventory and quote credits. It does not escrow GEN, represent money, or verify physical delivery. No frontend is authoritative. The deployer fixes participants, native endowments, two to four class criteria and immutable document commitments. Documents define the stated traits of the synthetic lots; source publication is not proof of offchain truth or issuer authenticity.

Anyone may trigger lot assessment while its participant remains unsealed. Leader and validators independently fetch complete commit-pinned JSON documents and verify SHA-256. Each derives every PASS, FAIL or UNKNOWN decision; validators require exact decision equality and independently audit each decision and supporting clauses against the complete text. Missing information stays UNKNOWN. Any FAIL blocks admission; otherwise any UNKNOWN requires REVIEW. Only an all-PASS lot moves its raw units into the tradable class balance. No confidence tolerance crosses an admission boundary. An assessment creates no certificate or consumable permission token.

Participants sign their own limit orders. ASK orders escrow admitted units; BID orders escrow quantity times limit credits. A participant takes only one side for the entire batch, including cancelled orders, preventing a single wallet from matching itself. This is not Sybil resistance. Orders can be cancelled by their unsealed owner. Each participant seals their own book. All seals enable permissionless clearing; any participant can instead abort before clearing, returning all open escrow, so withholding a seal cannot permanently lock balances. A participant may race clearing with an abort; final transaction order decides. There is no deadline or guaranteed execution.

Clearing evaluates all integer prices 1 through 100: maximize executable volume, minimize absolute eligible demand/supply imbalance, then choose the lowest price. With no executable volume, price is zero and every escrow is refunded. Within a side, better limit prices fill first and equal limits use earlier order ID. Bids and asks fill at the same price; unused reserves and unfilled inventory return atomically. All market orders become terminal. The final balance changes, not a status label, are the settlement. Afterwards participants may transfer admitted units among the configured wallets. Quote credits have no redemption or external value promise.

Conservation is checked after every balance-changing operation: raw units plus admitted balances plus open ASK escrow equal initial units; quote balances plus open BID reserves equal initial credits. Bounds are two to five participants, at most twelve lifetime orders, thirty raw units per participant, ten thousand initial quote credits per participant and a price ceiling of 100. Cancelling does not free an order slot. Unadmitted units remain raw and cannot enter orders or native transfers. Admission is irrevocable within the frozen batch; semantic consensus can be wrong.

This market controller, inventory conversion and escrow settlement are new implementation code. The CLI/receipt plumbing and Windows direct-test workaround reuse attributed MIT-licensed infrastructure. Uniform-price call auctions are established market mechanisms; see [Deutsche Börse's auction overview](https://www.cashmarket.deutsche-boerse.com/cash-en/trading/Xetra/continuous-trading-with-auctions) for maximum executable volume and minimum overhang. This contract's bounded grid and final lowest-price tie-break are its explicitly chosen policy, not a reproduction of the exchange rulebook. No new economic theorem, incentive compatibility or globally novel algorithm is claimed. Tests use independent exhaustive quantity-allocation checks, not just the same matching implementation twice.
